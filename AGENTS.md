# Repository Guidelines

## Project Vision

Halcyon is a Minecraft Java-edition minigame server built from scratch in Rust. Players should be able to enjoy a variety of minigames even when few human players are online, with humanlike bots filling games and providing engaging opponents and teammates.

Supporting very large bot populations is a core design requirement. The server must be highly efficient and scale vertically, making effective use of the CPU cores and memory available on a single machine.

The future target authoritative simulation rate is **120 TPS for worlds with up to 100 players**, counting humans and bots together, giving each simulation step an approximately **8.33 ms** deadline. This is a per-world performance target; the server may host multiple worlds and a larger total population. Preserve supported vanilla mechanics and their real-time timing; a higher scheduling rate must not make gameplay run six times faster. Network publication, bot decisions, and maintenance may have independent cadences. Budget for future gameplay systems and measure tail latency and missed deadlines, rather than spending the full deadline on basic player replication. Extremely crowded lobbies need a separately measured service budget; maintaining 120 TPS with 1,000 players in one lobby is not a current requirement. Production ground simulation and publication currently run at 20 TPS; 120 Hz is deferred to a separate performance milestone.

Halcyon does not aim for full feature parity with the official Minecraft server. Implement only the Minecraft features required by Halcyon's minigames, bots, and player experience. Design each feature around those needs rather than reproducing vanilla internals.

Implementation principles:

- Evaluate designs against their CPU, memory, and scheduling costs as player and bot populations grow.
- Prefer shared immutable data, reuse, batching, and bounded work where appropriate; substantiate performance claims with measurements.
- Preserve required client protocol compatibility while tailoring server behavior to Halcyon's needs.
- Treat the documented architecture as the current implementation, not a permanent constraint on vertical scaling.

## Current Implementation

Early-stage Minecraft Java-edition server in Rust. TCP accept on `127.0.0.1:25565` → per-connection packet loop. Implements authenticated encrypted login, configuration, a hub and on-demand game instances over maps imported from `worlds/`, bounded owner-managed block edits and chunk streaming. A 20 Hz owner shares ground/water collision, poses, surfaces and landing rules between client predictions and input-driven physics; publication is 20 Hz. Supports water and lava travel, currents, sprint swimming and dry crawling, including supported waterlogged static geometry and position-offset bamboo, dripstone and sulfur-spike collision, plus ladder/vine/scaffolding climbing, berry-bush/cobweb slowdown, powder-snow sinking and leather-boot walking, and bubble-column lift/drag. Closed shulker boxes, retained big-dripleaf tilt shapes and empty moving-piston placeholders use client-compatible collision; block-entity animation, scheduled dripleaf tilting, freezing and thorn damage remain unavailable. Sand/red sand/gravel fall, settle and can cause protected-rule-aware suffocation; players can escape settled overlaps. Rejected block predictions and collision mismatches resynchronize terrain. Players can open and close doors, trapdoors and fence gates. Other players see arm swings, hurt feedback and item-use hand state; a best-effort effect log delivers sounds, particles and entity animations to nearby observers. Includes reusable player lifecycle, validated inventory rearrangement and equipment synchronization. The hub keeps Survival editing protected; Creative placement and breaking are available for a supported arena-building catalog. Game rules can enable Survival placement and timed mining with default tool speeds; durability and drops stay disabled. Static signs retain front/back text, color and glow, and manual jukeboxes play vanilla discs and discard them on removal. Supported block entities are imported and delivered reliably, including chunk reloads. Void falls reset to spawn. Registry/block-state/item data targets 26.3. Vanilla 26.3 melee PvP between players covers reach, attack charge, criticals, sword sweeps, knockback and armor points/toughness. It is off in the protected hub unless `HALCYON_PVP=1` and opens during free-for-all fights; there are no enchantments, shields, durability, mace smashes or spear attacks. No drowning/air simulation, lava burning/damage, anticheat, compression, lighting engine, world saving or general block/fluid simulation yet.

Commands live in `crates/halcyon-server/src/commands/`, one command per file, with shared dispatch in `mod.rs`. `/gamemode <mode> [player]` accepts mode names or numeric IDs 0–3. `/tp <player>`, `/tp <target> <player>` and `/tp [target] <x> <y> <z>` support player names and world coordinates, including `~` offsets relative to the issuer; destinations must pass loaded-terrain and collision checks, except for spectators. Both commands offer native completion and are available to all players. Game modes synchronize to clients and persist through respawn. Creative enables client-toggled, collision-checked flight, invulnerability except void rules, and instant breaking through the bounded terrain overlay; paired halves and underlying water are retained correctly, with no drops. Sequenced block/item uses and mining actions share an eight-entry queue and four operations per player per step, with life-generation checks and authority resent before sequence acknowledgement. Spectators fly through terrain without collision or damage except void rules, cannot interact with blocks, items or inventory, are visible only to other spectators and hidden while viewing through another camera, and can follow a player's camera (attack to attach, sneak to leave; the owner snaps them to the target each step) or teleport through the spectator menu. Basic self-only Creative `/give <item> [count]` grants supported blocks, tools and discs without component syntax. Creative inventory acquisition remains unavailable. `/play <game>` joins an open instance of a game or starts one, and `/hub` returns to the hub.

- Source of truth: `https://minecraft.wiki/w/Java_Edition_protocol/Packets` — authoritative for packet IDs, states, field order/types.
- Content is very large: NEVER dump full page; always trim after fetch to the exact packet/state section needed.

## Architecture & Data Flow

Flow: `TcpListener.incoming()` → `Client{stream, ctx, server: Arc<Server>}` → bounded receive buffer → `read_packet(buf, &ctx)` → `Packets::read` matched on `(packet_id, ctx.state)` → state handler. Dispatch uses the connection state because payload types can be shared across states. Dynamic packets encode into a reusable buffer; cached configuration frames and chunk parts bypass repeated encoding. Ciphertext persists across partial writes and `Pending`, and sends flush explicitly.

Crate dependency: `halcyon-server → mc_protocol → macros` (proc-macro), plus `halcyon-server → halcyon-world` and `halcyon-server → halcyon-commands → halcyon-commands-macros`. Protocol owns codecs; macros owns `#[packet]` codegen; server owns I/O, lifecycle, wire caches and chunk scheduling; world owns disk parsing and immutable pre-encoded chunk sections. The command library owns typed syntax, usage, completion hooks and a transport-neutral tree; its proc-macro crate provides `CommandSet`, `CommandArgs` and `CommandValue` derives. Declare each command's arguments in its server command file; keep player lookup, permissions and execution in the server. The server adapts the derived tree to Minecraft packets and caches it at startup.

`MapCatalog` loads every folder in `worlds/` once; each `Map` shares its immutable `World` and encoded chunks with every instance of it. `HALCYON_HUB_MAP`, `HALCYON_FFA_MAP` and `HALCYON_BOXES_MAP` pick folder names (default `Parkour Spiral`); `boxes` builds its glass duel boxes over its map through `Game::on_create` and `GameCtx::set_blocks`. An executor-local `Instance` owns player state, an `Arena` terrain overlay and a `Game`, simulates at 20 Hz and publishes every 50 ms. The owner runs `Game::on_join` during admission, before the client learns its entity ID, and drains `GameEvent`s (death, respawn, departure) into the game after each step; games act through `GameCtx`. The per-executor `InstanceManager` creates, matchmakes and reaps instances; a game that calls `close()` stops once empty. Entity IDs come from one process-wide `EntityIds` space and accounts from one `Server` claim set, so a player keeps its Play Login ID across instances. A game sends a player elsewhere with a `Destination` in its `PlayerUpdate`: the client drops its session, removes its tab roster, sends `Respawn` through a placeholder `halcyon:limbo` level back into `minecraft:overworld` (the client only rebuilds its level when the name changes) and re-registers under the same ID. `Arc<Server>` retains immutable startup resources. Each `Client` holds a unique session guard, player `Observer`, and `WorldObserver` with delivered terrain/entity baselines. Cached base chunks are followed by current patches before entity admission; removals precede terrain unload/replacement. Collision, suffocation and replication read the same `BlockView`. Registry borrows end before socket I/O. Dropping the guard signals cleanup without needing registration queue space. Independent instance ownership is the boundary for future executor placement; every shard (`shard.rs`) binds the address with SO_REUSEPORT, runs a permanent home hub plus any game instances, and serves only connections whose player is in one of them. `InstanceManager::route` (used at login with `Destination::Hub` and by `/play`) fills the fullest open instance of the kind on any shard (this one on ties), else starts one on the least-loaded shard (this one on ties); hubs hold `HALCYON_HUB_CAPACITY` players (default 100, bots included) and overflow hubs stop after 60 s empty. Play Login draws the entity ID before placement, so a first placement on another shard hands off without a Respawn, using the `Directory` each shard publishes every 250 ms. A cross-shard move leaves the instance and sends both respawns on the old shard, then parks the connection (`TcpStream::into_accepted`, cipher state, unread plaintext, keepalive and chunk-batch state, account claim) into a 64-entry `channel_mesh` handoff queue; the new shard rebinds the socket and admits the player under the same entity ID. A full queue sends the player to a hub on the old shard. Startup refuses an address another listener holds, since SO_REUSEPORT would share it silently. Bots run on shard 0 and follow game destinations within it; `HALCYON_BOT_GAME=<kind>` makes them queue for that game from the hub repeatedly, with a melee brain (`bot/fighter.rs`) outside the home hub. Gameplay state never crosses shards.

Add a packet: define `#[packet(State, 0xNN, Client|Server)] struct` in `crates/mc-protocol/src/packets/{server,client}/mod.rs` → add `Packets` enum variant → add `(id, state)` arm in `Packets::read` → handle in `crates/halcyon-server/src/states/<state>.rs`.

## Key Directories

- `crates/halcyon-server/src/`: runtime — `main.rs`, `server.rs`, `client/mod.rs`, `transport.rs`, `packet.rs`, `lifecycle.rs`, `player.rs`, `combat.rs`, `effects.rs`, `movement/mod.rs`, `movement/physics.rs`, `movement/water.rs`, `movement/media.rs`, `simulation.rs`, `inventory/mod.rs`, `arena.rs`, `interaction/mod.rs`, `world_sync.rs`, `map.rs`, `shard.rs`, `game/mod.rs`, `instance/mod.rs`, `instance/manager.rs`, `visibility/mod.rs`, `cache.rs`, `chunks.rs`, `states/`, `auth/`, `encryption/`.
- `crates/halcyon-world/src/`: startup world loading — `loader/mod.rs`, section palette encoding in `loader/section.rs`, `disk_nbt.rs`, `blockstates.rs`, `openings.rs`, `terrain.rs`, generated `collision_data.rs`, immutable world grid in `lib.rs`.
- `crates/halcyon-commands/src/`: reusable command syntax — `lib.rs` traits and usage example, `argument.rs` built-in word/player/numeric arguments, `position.rs` world coordinates, `tree.rs` command graph. `CommandArgs` structs define positional fields; enums define alternative forms.
- `crates/halcyon-commands-macros/src/`: derive macros and compile-time declaration checks.
- `crates/mc-protocol/src/`: codec — `encode.rs`, `decode.rs`, `error.rs`, `state.rs`, `packets/mod.rs`, `packets/{client,server}/`, `types/{varint,int,str,bytes,uuid,game_profile}.rs`, `data/{motd,config/*}`, `auth/`.
- `crates/mc-protocol-macros/src/`: `lib.rs`, `attrs.rs`, `format.rs` — `#[packet]` / `Payload` / `InnerPacket` codegen.
- `docs/`: vanilla feature inventory, Halcyon feature assessment, `feature-progress.md` implementation tracker, and `minigames.md` game framework guide with its capability tracker. Keep both trackers current when feature or `GameCtx` support changes; describe what is available, not implementation details. No `scripts/`, `tools/`, standalone `tests/`, `benches/`, `examples/`, `.github/`, or `Dockerfile`.
- `crates/halcyon-server/src/bench/`: opt-in component benchmarks, compiled only for tests.

Server modules group responsibilities in directories: `client/` owns gameplay delivery, publication and writing; `instance/` owns sessions, registry management and scheduling; `movement/` owns collision, terrain contact and physics; `visibility/` owns frame encoding and delivery; `inventory/click.rs` owns menu operations. Player login/profile packet codecs live in `mc-protocol/src/packets/client/player.rs`. Unit tests live in each crate's `src/tests/tests_<feature>.rs`, with local guidance in `src/tests/AGENTS.md`. Wire them into the feature module with `#[cfg(test)]` and `#[path = "..."] mod tests;` to preserve access to private internals. Keep generated data tables intact.

## Development Commands

Stock Cargo, no wrappers/scripts:

```sh
cargo run -p halcyon-server          # binary `halcyon` -> target/debug/halcyon
cargo build --workspace
cargo test --workspace
cargo fmt --all -- --check
cargo clippy --workspace --all-targets -- -D warnings
```

The protocol macro crate is an automatic workspace member through its path dependency; the command crates are explicit members. Workspace checks include all of them.

## Code Conventions & Common Patterns

- Formatting: `rustfmt.toml` (`single_line_if_else_max_width=60`, `single_line_let_else_max_width=60`, `use_small_heuristics="Max"`). Run `cargo fmt` before yielding.
- Module layout: use `feature/mod.rs` when a module has a `feature/` directory for submodules; do not keep both `feature.rs` and `feature/`. Leaf modules can remain `feature.rs`.
- Codec traits: `PacketWriter{read, write, body_len}`, `Payload{const ID, const STATE, payload_len, decode_payload, write_payload, write_packet}`, `InnerPacket{id(), state()}`. Zero-copy decode borrows `&'p str` / `&[u8]` from read buffer.
- Packet declaration: `#[packet(Login, 0x00, Client)]` + field `#[format="varint"|"json"]`. Direction files: `packets/server/mod.rs` = client→server, `packets/client/mod.rs` = server→client.
- Naming: packets `PascalCase` + `*Request/*Response` (`ServerListPing`, `LoginStart`, `EncryptionRequest`); methods `handle_packet/read_packet/write_packet/payload_len/decode_payload/write_payload`; fields `snake_case` (`verify_token`, `next_state`); consts `UPPER_SNAKE` (`ADDR`, `SIZE`); traits `PacketWriter/Payload/InnerPacket`.
- State: `Context` holds wire state and profile data. Handshake decodes `HandshakeIntent`, never arbitrary `State`. `LoginProgress` guards authentication and acknowledgement. Handlers own transitions through Login → Config → Play. `compression` is unused; do not negotiate it until framing supports it.
- Async: one `glommio` executor (shard) per core from `LocalExecutorPoolBuilder` with `MaxSpread`, capped by `HALCYON_SHARDS`; one local task per connection on its instance's shard. Receive storage starts at 1024 bytes and grows to at most `MAX_FRAME_LEN + 3`. A complete frame with an incomplete payload is invalid, not a partial network read. Parse loops and chunk sends yield explicitly; blocking HTTPS runs off the executor with a request timeout.
- Scheduling: login/config deadline 60 s, writes 10 s, Play keepalive every 15 s with a 30 s response deadline. Chunk batches wait for acknowledgement, respect bounded client rate feedback, and cap view distance at 8. Residency distinguishes real terrain and empty padding. Keep configuration/chunk encodings shared; lighting suffix is shared separately to avoid duplicating 104 KiB per cached chunk.
- Multiplayer: at most 1,024 reserved/active humans and 256 pending registrations; this is an admission bound, not a measured network capacity. Every teleport confirmation has a 30 s deadline and a non-droppable acknowledgement mailbox. Movement has 32 ordered proposals/player, four processed/step, with collision-cell budgets; overflow corrects to accepted authority. Action queues are separately bounded; respawn requests coalesce outside them. Missed publication ticks are skipped; simulation catches up at most four steps. Observer output is bounded to 256 KiB/2,048 protocol frames, with 16 player admissions per publication and drain slices of 32 frames/64 KiB. Entity removals precede terrain unload/replacement; empty padding never enables entity spawns. Tab profiles persist throughout instance membership and respawn. Relative movement uses Java-compatible `Math.round(position * 4096)` deltas against delivered positions. Cosmetic effects are encoded once into a 512-entry instance ring; each connection reads it with its own cursor, at most 16 per drain slice and ahead of player frames, and skips what it missed instead of reserving observer capacity. Swings coalesce per step outside the action queue.
- Combat: attacks queue as player actions and resolve after every player advanced, like spectator cameras; `combat.rs` holds the pure rules. Human knockback is sent as client velocity (the server keeps none); bots receive it as motion.
- Gameplay: validate collision/synchronization, not speed, flight or jump trajectories. Unsupported movement contexts are rejected. Dry surfaces execute target-version friction, speed, jump, bounce, honey-slide and landing rules. Water and lava share one height, immersion and current pass charged to the movement cell budget; lava halves fall distance and uses overworld (slow) currents; active swimming is separate from the swimming/crawling pose. Chunk section fluid counts and configuration fluid tags must identify water to the 26.3 client. Sequenced block/item interactions queue at most 8 per player and process 4 per step; terrain and inventory corrections precede acknowledgements, and held-slot changes retain their received order. Shape/support transactions allow 64 cells and 512 checks, rejecting oversized chains atomically. Supported block entities have at most 128 retained records per column, including removal records, and at most 256 jukeboxes play simultaneously. Arena edits allow at most 16,384 changed cells, 1,024 per column; queue/process at most 64 trusted edits per step. Sand/gravel work has 16,384 scheduled positions, 64 starts/step and 1,024 active falls. Unsupported falling routes are rejected before source removal; contextual geometry and special entity motion media remain disabled. No full-world hot-path scans. Inventory predictions never grant stacks. Crafting, external containers, drops, nested storage interactions and Creative inventory acquisition are disabled. Game APIs deliberately enable damage or replace loadouts; death retains inventory/cursor by default. Item components support bounded presentation overrides, not gameplay effects.
- DI: `Arc<Server>` cloned into `Client::new(stream, server)`; handlers reach keys via `client.server.keys`.
- Inventory slots: use checked `InventorySlot` menu indices and `HotbarSlot` selections from `inventory/slot.rs` in gameplay APIs and state. Convert raw packet coordinates and mode/button combinations with `InventoryAction::from_click/from_selection`; queued clicks use `InventoryClick` operations, `MouseButton` and `SwapHand`, while outside pickups use `ClickTarget::Outside`. Slot identity does not grant access to crafting or override placement rules; the owner validates placement and drag gesture state. Convert back to wire numbers only when encoding packets.
- Error handling: typed decode/encode/connection errors; malformed client input ends only that connection. Validate lengths before allocation. Unknown packets consume their frame without logging. Set `HALCYON_DIAGNOSTICS=1` for connection failures and five-second aggregate lobby/observer metrics; avoid unconditional per-packet or per-chunk output.

## Runtime/Tooling Preferences

- Runtime: Rust `edition = "2024"`, `resolver = "3"`, no pinned toolchain/MSRV — ambient stable. Package manager: Cargo, `Cargo.lock` committed.
- Linux-only (`glommio-ng 0.12` io_uring); build needs system OpenSSL headers (`openssl 0.10`).
- No Node/Bun/Python/Docker/CI. Do not add JS tooling.
- `.gitignore` is deny-by-default (`*` + `!*/`), with explicit exceptions for Rust, manifests, root configuration, and this file. New documentation, scripts, fixtures or CI files need whitelist entries.
- The protocol macro crate is automatically included in the workspace despite not appearing in the root `members` list.

## Testing & QA

- Minimal tests: the repo should not be lost in a test soup. tests should only be added when required, for small units. They should not verify the entire server working, only small chunks.
- Live-client verification: `prismlauncher -l <instance> -s 127.0.0.1:25565` is allowed for test runs; choose an instance matching the current packet/registry version. Always close the client and the server afterward.
- QA = `cargo fmt --check` + `cargo clippy --workspace --all-targets`.
- Target-world benchmark: `cargo test -p halcyon-server --release target_world_benchmark -- --ignored --nocapture`. Uses 100 synthetic players and 100 encrypted memory sinks, shared production writer budgets, idle/spread/crowded/visibility-churn cases and a hypothetical 120 Hz driver. Reports CPU p95/p99, samples exceeding 8.33 ms, delivery lag, bytes and payload-capacity estimates. Set `HALCYON_BENCH_REPORT=/tmp/halcyon-bench.json` for machine-readable results and optionally `HALCYON_BENCH_MAX_P99_US=4000` for a hardware-specific performance gate. It excludes sockets, authentication, chunks, executor scheduling and gameplay physics; the production publication clock remains 20 Hz.
- Gameplay benchmark: `cargo test -p halcyon-server --release gameplay_world_benchmark -- --ignored --nocapture`. Runs retained terrain, production 20 Hz rules/publication and 100 encrypted sinks, including ground/water motion, swimming/crawling, cobweb/scaffolding/powder-snow/bubble-column travel, slab traversal, lifecycle/equipment churn and repeated 32-block sand/gravel falls. Player and world replication share bounded writer slices, with up to 16 fair slices per owner step. Reports simulation/combined p95/p99, misses, corrections, delivery lag and payload estimates, including terrain/entity overlay and world-observer payloads. The same report/gate environment variables apply; `HALCYON_BENCH_SCENARIO` selects one named gameplay case. `gameplay_scheduler_benchmark` combines these features with real Glommio pacing (about 11 s; needs io_uring permission). Measurements use a 50 ms deadline and do not establish socket, 1,024-falling-entity or 120 Hz capacity.
- Larger synthetic stress measurement: `cargo test -p halcyon-server --release synthetic_lobby -- --ignored --nocapture`. Uses 16 unencrypted memory sinks and populations through 4,096, with unrestricted drains; its results are not directly comparable to the target-world benchmark.
- Bot combat bench: `cargo test -p halcyon-server --release combat_duel_benchmark -- --ignored --nocapture`. Headless duels under the real owner rules in a `boxes` box: strength ladder, mirrors, entity-order bias, technique ablations and humanlike tells (`TELL:` lines). `tune_tactics` searches the fighter's `MASTER` tactics and `trace_duel` prints one duel tick by tick; see `docs/bots.md` §9.
- Add deterministic, focused correctness tests and representative benchmark cases as gameplay mechanics arrive. Timing gates are opt-in because results depend on hardware and system load; keep a small live-client check for rendering and prediction compatibility.
